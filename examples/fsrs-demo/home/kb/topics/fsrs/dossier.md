## 1. Orientation

FSRS replaces SM-2's single "ease" number with a small model of memory for each card — [[retrievability]], [[stability]] and [[difficulty]] — fitted to your own review history, and it predicts recall far better than SM-2 does; whether that saves you reviews in practice is shown so far only by simulation.

What matters most:

- **The model.** Recall probability decays along a power-law [[forgetting-curve]] (its steepness became trainable per user in FSRS-6); stability is "days until recall drops to 90%". A successful review multiplies stability by a factor that shrinks for hard cards and already-stable memories and grows when the review came late. See [[stability-increase]].
- **The training.** Maximum likelihood (log loss) with backpropagation through time over each card's history ([[parameter-optimisation]]); defaults fitted on ~10k users are used below a few hundred reviews.
- **The control.** You set [[desired-retention]]; workload rises steeply toward 100%. Grades must be honest: Hard counts as a success ([[grading]]).
- **The evidence.** On the developers' public benchmark (~10k Anki users), FSRS-6 out-predicts half-life regression and a constant baseline, and — in the last version that still listed it (August 2025) — Anki's SM-2 by a wide margin (log loss 0.346 vs 0.616). Anki's SM-2 even lost to the constant baseline. See [[benchmark-metrics]] and [[sm2]].

**Caveats that matter:** SM-2 doesn't predict recall natively, so its benchmark probabilities were constructed; all performance evidence comes from the FSRS developers (public, reproducible, not independent); the SM-17 comparison covers 19 users; and rankings of weaker models flip depending on whether users are weighted by review count.

**Open:** does better prediction mean fewer reviews for real learners? The "20–30% fewer reviews" figure is simulated, and its setup isn't documented in the sources read.

## 2. Main concepts

- **[Desired retention](../../concepts/desired-retention.md)** — The recall probability you want when a card comes due — the one setting to tune; workload rises steeply toward 100%. _(builds on `forgetting-curve`)_
- **[Difficulty](../../concepts/difficulty.md)** — How hard it is to raise a card's stability — not how hard it felt. _(builds on `dsr-model`)_
- **[Fitting parameters to your review log](../../concepts/parameter-optimisation.md)** — The optimiser fits FSRS's parameters to your history by maximum likelihood (minimising log loss) with backpropagation through time. _(builds on `forgetting-curve`, `stability-increase`)_
- **[How a review changes stability](../../concepts/stability-increase.md)** — After a success, stability grows less for hard cards and already-stable memories, and more when recall odds were low (the spacing effect). _(builds on `stability`, `difficulty`, `retrievability`)_
- **[How schedulers are compared](../../concepts/benchmark-metrics.md)** — Log loss and RMSE(bins) test calibration, AUC tests discrimination; beating the constant AVG baseline is the minimum. _(related `parameter-optimisation`)_
- **[Memory stability](../../concepts/stability.md)** — Days until recall probability falls from 100% to 90%. _(builds on `dsr-model`; contrasts `retrievability`)_
- **[Retrievability](../../concepts/retrievability.md)** — The probability you can recall a card right now; it falls every day without review. _(builds on `dsr-model`)_
- **[SM-2, Anki's legacy scheduler](../../concepts/sm2.md)** — One ease factor per card multiplies the interval; no explicit recall probability, nothing fitted to you. _(contrasts `dsr-model`)_
- **[The DSR memory model](../../concepts/dsr-model.md)** — FSRS tracks each card with three numbers — retrievability, stability, difficulty — instead of SM-2's single ease factor. _(contrasts `sm2`)_
- **[The power-law forgetting curve](../../concepts/forgetting-curve.md)** — Recall odds drop fast before the stability point and flatly after it; FSRS-6 learns the curve's shape per user. _(builds on `retrievability`, `stability`)_
- **[What your grades mean to FSRS](../../concepts/grading.md)** — Again means forgot; Hard means recalled. Using Hard for a failure inflates intervals. _(related `parameter-optimisation`)_

  - vs [cl-20261028-y4g7](../../claims/cl-20261028-y4g7.md): Weighted by number of reviews, trainable Anki-SM-2 predicts recall slightly better than half-life regression (log loss 0.407 vs 0.415).

## 6. Important uncertainties

- On the benchmark's last version that included SM-2 (August 2025, ~10k Anki users), FSRS-6 predicted recall far better than Anki's SM-2 — log loss 0.346 vs 0.616 per user (0.321 vs 0.490 weighted by reviews) — and Anki's SM-2 did worse than the constant-average baseline (0.395); FSRS-6 with default parameters (0.366) also beat it. ([cl-20261028-5csf](../../claims/cl-20261028-5csf.md)) — SM-2 doesn't predict recall natively; the benchmark built its probabilities from assumed forgetting curves, so this measures prediction calibration, not review workload. Same organisation as FSRS; SM-2 rows were removed from the README in Aug 2025.
- In the FSRS author's comparison on 19 SuperMemo users, FSRS-6 had lower prediction error than SuperMemo's SM-17 (average universal metric 0.029 vs 0.044). ([cl-20261028-bqqv](../../claims/cl-20261028-bqqv.md)) — 19 users, the author's own implementation of SuperMemo's metric (code unreleased by SuperMemo), not independent.
- The two rankings differ because weighting by reviews favours users with long histories ('best case' performance); trainable SM-2 benefits more from plenty of data than HLR does — so 'which is better' depends on how much history a learner has. ([cl-20261028-e2bn](../../claims/cl-20261028-e2bn.md)) — An interpretation of two tables; per-user data would settle it.
- For overdue reviews, FSRS's gain in stability levels off at an upper limit instead of growing with the delay as in SM-2. ([cl-20261028-hf86](../../claims/cl-20261028-hf86.md)) — One source (the authors' specification); not checked against data here.
- RMSE (bins) is measured in probability units, so FSRS-6's predicted recall is off by roughly 6.5 percentage points across bins, against about 10 for the constant baseline and about 17 for Anki's SM-2 (August 2025): gaps that look small in log loss are large in calibration terms. ([cl-20261028-qg95](../../claims/cl-20261028-qg95.md)) — RMSE(bins) averages over bins weighted by sample size; individual cards can be off by much more. It still says nothing direct about workload.
- Personalised parameters need at least a few hundred reviews; with fewer, FSRS uses default parameters fitted on roughly 10,000 users. ([cl-20261028-zy9f](../../claims/cl-20261028-zy9f.md)) — 'A few hundred' is the manual's rule of thumb, not a measured threshold.

### Answered (9)

- [q-20261028-2ykh](../../questions/q-20261028-2ykh.md) How does FSRS relate to half-life regression and the SSP-MMC research it came from? _(plan, lineage)_
  - result: FSRS comes from MaiMemo's DHP model and the SSP-MMC work (KDD 2022); HLR (Duolingo, ACL 2016) is an earlier trainable model used there as a baseline and still in the benchmark [cl-20261028-wpgw].
- [q-20261028-6zpd](../../questions/q-20261028-6zpd.md) Is there a published head-to-head comparison of FSRS and SM-2 on the same review data (e.g. earlier srs-benchmark results or the fsrs-vs-sm15/sm17 repositories)? _(gap, evidence)_
  - result: Yes: the srs-benchmark README included SM-2 and Anki-SM-2 until 2025-08-22. FSRS-6 log loss 0.346 vs Anki-SM-2 0.616 per user; Anki-SM-2 was worse than the constant baseline [cl-20261028-5csf]. Caveat: SM-2's recall probabilities were constructed for the benchmark. Against SM-17 the only comparison is the author's own, on 19 users [cl-20261028-bqqv].
- [q-20261028-d4z8](../../questions/q-20261028-d4z8.md) What are FSRS's memory variables (difficulty, stability, retrievability) and how does each update after a review? _(user, memory-model)_
  - result: Three per-card variables: R (recall probability now), S (days until R hits 90%), D (how hard it is to raise S) [cl-20261028-6qtn]. R decays daily along the forgetting curve; S and D change only at reviews. A success multiplies S by a factor that is smaller for high D and high S and larger when R was low [cl-20261028-frr9]; a lapse uses a separate post-lapse formula.
- [q-20261028-epmj](../../questions/q-20261028-epmj.md) What does desired retention control, and how does it trade recall against workload? _(plan, practice)_
  - result: It is the target recall probability at the due date; higher means shorter intervals and more reviews, steeply so near 100% [cl-20261028-68cf].
- [q-20261028-f9zj](../../questions/q-20261028-f9zj.md) Check “How schedulers are compared” — the user is not convinced: Log loss gaps look small (0.35 vs 0.39). Is that a meaningful difference for a learner? _(learning)_
  - result: Yes, the gap matters. RMSE(bins) is in probability units: FSRS-6 is off by ~6.5 percentage points, the constant baseline ~10, Anki's SM-2 ~17 [cl-20261028-qg95]. Log loss looks compressed because most reviews are easy to predict. It still isn't a workload measurement.
- [q-20261028-sbtg](../../questions/q-20261028-sbtg.md) What does a log-loss difference like 0.346 vs 0.395 mean in practice for a learner's workload or retention? _(gap, evidence)_
  - result: Read RMSE(bins) as percentage points of calibration error: ~6.5 (FSRS-6) vs ~10 (constant) vs ~17 (Anki SM-2) [cl-20261028-qg95].
- [q-20261028-y3y3](../../questions/q-20261028-y3y3.md) What forgetting-curve formula does FSRS use, and why that shape? _(plan, memory-model)_
  - result: R(t,S) = (1 + factor·t/S)^(−decay), a power curve tuned so R = 90% at t = S; decay was 1 in v4, 0.5 in FSRS-4.5 and is trainable per user in FSRS-6 [cl-20261028-dgxb].
- [q-20261028-y8dw](../../questions/q-20261028-y8dw.md) How are the FSRS parameters optimised from a review log, and what loss is minimised? _(user, training)_
  - result: Maximum likelihood on recall outcomes (log loss) with backpropagation through time over each card's grade/interval history; S and D are latent [cl-20261028-23u2].
- [q-20261028-z3cx](../../questions/q-20261028-z3cx.md) On what data and metrics does FSRS beat SM-2, and by how much? _(user, evidence)_
  - result: Prediction quality: FSRS-6 beats HLR, Ebisu and AVG on the current benchmark [cl-20261028-v4er] and beat Anki's SM-2 by a wide margin on the Aug 2025 version [cl-20261028-5csf]. Workload savings ('20–30% fewer reviews') come only from the authors' simulation (open question q-20261028-6mr7).
